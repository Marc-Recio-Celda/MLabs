# AXIOMS — the rules that may never be violated

> **Version:** 1.0.0
> the first tagged release is stage 2's exit condition (AX-20).

> **Level 2 of three.** Above this file, `PHILOSOPHY.md` says what the company optimises for and
> breaks every tie. Below it, **decisions** are instance-level and live in the instance's own
> log, in a parseable format carrying author, date, reasoning and what was discarded. This file
> holds neither purpose nor record: **only the rules, and each one names the clause it serves.**
>
> **Who touches this file.** The superauditor checks these are not violated and never edits
> them. R&D proposes changes to them — this is the layer R&D works on when the work is improving
> the company. The operator decides. Nothing here may amend `PHILOSOPHY.md`.
>
> **Append-only, and identifiers are never reused or renumbered.** A retired axiom keeps its
> number and becomes a **⚫** row naming what absorbed it or where it went; new ones append. A
> renumbering would break every citation in every department at once, and the gap in the
> sequence is information — it says something was here and where it went.
> **Status:** 🟢 in force · 🟡 proposed · ⚫ retired.
> **Anchoring:** a bare `AX-n` inside this repo is unambiguous and stays bare; the moment it
> leaves it carries its anchor (AX-23), and an instance's or a project's own `AX-n` series is a
> different series resolved the same way.
>
> **How a rule gets here.** Not by being written down — by surviving a test:
>
> > **Would this still bind an instance that is not this one — a different person, a different
> > body of knowledge, different projects, a different toolchain?**
>
> **Distilled from 109 private decisions by that test, and kept short by the review that
> follows.** The ratio is the
> point: a log where everything is a rule has no hierarchy, and a system that cannot tell its
> load-bearing rules from its housekeeping protects the wrong ones. What the test rejects is not
> lost — it stays private, which is where it was always true. Each axiom points at its origin as
> `NEXUS:M-nn`; those pointers resolve only for the operator, and the originals never move.
>
> **The set is kept honest in both directions by the promotion review** (`skills/superauditor/`),
> which the operator calls — around every twenty decisions is the cadence worth aiming at — to
> ask what is missing, what is contradicted, and, the direction that stops inflation, **what is
> dead**: an axiom whose violation nobody would notice.
>
> **Adding an axiom reviews the whole set, never just the new one.** A set saturates one entry
> at a time, and no single addition ever looks like the one that did it; the only moment the
> interaction is visible is when something changes. So a change here fires the review over
> **every axiom in every department**, and the question asked of each is not *is this true* but
> **is this still load-bearing.** An axiom that turns out to be narrower or softer than it looked
> **becomes a decision** — that is a normal outcome, costs nothing, and is the mechanism by which
> this file stays short.
>
> **Departments, not ranks.** Company axioms live here; **instance axioms live in the operations
> centre**; project axioms live in their project. They differ in **scope, not in force** — inside
> its own jurisdiction each binds equally, none overrides another, and **they are read and
> reviewed together**, because a rule can only contradict another rule it shares a reader with.

| **AX-1** | 🟢 | **Structure separates from state, and what is public carries no personal data.** The methodology — hierarchy, roles, skills, templates — is one artefact, public by release and depersonalised by construction. **Everything an operator actually works on — knowledge, projects, logs, decisions, work in flight — lives in NEXUS, the operations centre**: one private artefact per instance, a sibling of the structure, which the public one never contains. | **AX-12** | 🟢 | **The governing document states what the system is for, and what it refuses, before it states any mechanism.** A charter that defines only mechanism gives no way to decide a case it did not foresee — and purpose is what breaks ties when two rules both apply. **The failure this prevents is measurable and was measured:** across one instance's most recent 39 rules, the conduct section was cited nine times and the purpose once. | **AX-13** | 🟢 | **Verification never shares a role with generation.** A single role holding both stances drifts toward the generative one, and a gate cannot be owned by the party producing the work. | **AX-14** | 🟢 | **Allocate work by what each access point is entitled to claim, not by what it can see.** Two independent axes fix the strongest available claim: *can it read the source* and *can it execute*. An access point given copies may claim only what it was given; one that reads the source may claim what is there; only one that can run things may claim that something passes. Two consequences: **what is handed to an executor is never the edit but the verification, stated as a prediction** — the executor returns the real output and the gap is the finding; and **the executor may refuse.** An executor that finds a task's premise false against what it actually sees stops and says so rather than executing. **This is why every task carries its why, not only its what** — an executor that does not know the purpose has nothing to test the premise against. That is an obligation, not a discretion: whoever plans cannot see the state and whoever executes can, so the executor is the last point at which a divergence can be stopped. | PH-4 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-63` · `M-82` · `M-49` |
| **AX-15** | 🟢 | **The two channels are mirrors, and nobody empties the queue they fill.** An inbox runs from agent to operator; a task list runs from operator to agent; in each, the party that removes an item is never the party that added it. | **AX-16** | 🟢 | **Completed work leaves the live list.** A finished task is deleted, not marked done — a list that only grows stops saying what is left — and its trace becomes a line in a dated, append-only log. **Every line of that log cites an artefact**, or it is not written: checkable detail, not more detail. | PH-6 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-42` · `M-99` · `M-88` |
| **AX-17** | 🟢 | **Three destinations by commitment, not by topic — and everything routes at the moment it is written.** Decided → the decision log. | **AX-18** | 🟢 | **The gate is set by the origin of the change; its shape by the channel.** What the operator originated is transcribed and logged without ceremony; **what the agent originated always goes through the gate** — and the notice is unconditional, never conditioned on the change seeming substantive, because that judgement is made by the interested party. Reversibility is never grounds to relax it: version control solves irreversibility, not divergence. **The shape follows the channel:** asynchronous through the inbox where the agent cannot reach the operator in the same turn, synchronous in session where it can — and because a synchronous gate loses the delay and the second reading, a change passing through one writes its log line in the same act. **What must be gated is a mechanical test**, not a feeling: it touches a protected file, it creates or retires an artefact, or it changes a written rule. | PH-4 · PH-2 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-34` · `M-59` · `M-64` |
| **AX-19** | 🟢 | **The permission model needs a class whose question is ownership, not risk.** Work the operator does not own is never modified — however dead it looks — and observations about it go to its owners' inbox instead. The other classes all describe things the operator or the agent owns, so none of them can catch this; the failure it prevents is an agent correctly evaluating an artefact's usefulness and never evaluating its ownership. Autonomy applies on one's own branch only, and the conduct that goes with the class **loads from a flag rather than sitting in the core rules** (AX-4). | PH-5 · PH-3 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-103` |
| **AX-20** | 🟢 | **A copy is either a generated view or a declared photograph — there is no third kind.** A **view** is regenerated from its source, cannot diverge, and loses on conflict; where a consumer can reach the source it reads it and keeps no copy. A **photograph** diverges on purpose and nobody updates it, so it is legitimate only when it carries three things: the date it was frozen, why, and **which copy wins**. Anything that is neither is a duplicate, and duplicates are debt with an expiry date or they are defects. **Generated output is byte-identical for identical input** — no run-time timestamps — so drift is detected by comparing content and history carries no empty diffs. **And every artefact stamps the version of the rule set it was written against:** without that line there is no way to tell a current document from one nobody has reviewed, and drift stays invisible at zero maintenance cost. | PH-5 · PH-3 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-66` · `M-97` · `M-33` · `M-47` |
| **AX-21** | 🟢 | **Startup reads state; knowledge loads on demand; anything opened beyond the index's row is a defect in the index.** At session start an agent reads where the work stands, what was decided and what is pending, from a literal path — never the body of knowledge, which is read when the reasoning touches it. **A routing index says not only what exists but when to read it**, and there is exactly one such map. **An agent declares every file it had to open outside the row it was pointed to**, and that gap is logged as a repair to the index, never as licence to read less: economy is bought by routing better, never by tracing less. **Rules travel, maps point** — whatever must survive a fresh checkout of one repository alone is generated *into* it; whatever would go stale if copied is a pointer. | PH-6 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-79` · `M-52` · `M-67` · `M-53` |
| **AX-22** | 🟢 | **Evidence comes before the argument, and before the deletion.** A decision resting on a checkable claim runs the command that would falsify it **before** anything is built on top; *"this rests on no checkable claim"* is a valid answer. Calibrate on the premise that feels obvious — that is the one nobody instruments. **Measure only freshly fetched data, and name which copy you measured**, or the check launders a stale reading into a measured claim. **Deleting anything requires the search that found no consumer, pasted with its output** — test coverage is not that proof, because coverage says what is tested, not what is used; if a change alters what any caller receives it is a redesign and needs a logged decision. **A session closes by running the checks and only then publishing, in that order** — and an agent that could not run them says so, because one that cannot verify and stays silent is indistinguishable from one whose checks passed. | PH-4 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-84` · `M-91` · `M-81` · `M-85` · `M-51` · `M-70` |
| **AX-23** | 🟢 | **Nothing is deleted; it is located.** Every deliberate exclusion — an ignore rule, a retired file, a detail summarised instead of kept — **names the address where the original still lives**. Deleting inside a version-controlled repository is not loss, because history is the raw layer; what loses data is an exclusion with no address. *"It is somewhere on disk"* is a claim with no check. | PH-3 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-80` |
| **AX-24** | 🟢 | **The log records what has no other record, and its fields can only be filled while deciding.** Version history says what changed and when; the log says **why**, **who originated it** — a person, `joint`, `inherited`, `agent` — and **what was discarded**, which has no other trace anywhere because it was never written. Never narrate the diff. **`inherited` is the load-bearing value**: a constraint you did not choose can only be discovered to be wrong, and writing it as your own decision hides the line between what is renegotiable and what is not. **A discarded thing keeps its entry and its status**, or the same proposal returns in six months with nobody able to say it was already answered. **Every field here is decision-time-only** — which sources informed it, who proposed it, what was rejected exist in the author's head at that moment and nowhere afterwards. A field that may legitimately be empty is written empty; a field left absent is indistinguishable from a real zero. | PH-3 · PH-4 | inherited — `NEXUS:M-41` · `M-55` · `M-77` · `M-100` |
| **AX-25** | 🟢 | **Passive detection: record what you cross, one line, and neither investigate nor fix it.** An agent that stumbles on an inconsistency while doing something else logs it and moves on. | **AX-28** | 🟢 | **Removing a decision from the operator requires a logged waiver naming what is given up**, and the waiver is what makes it reversible when the trade's review date arrives. **A change the operator cannot restate unaided does not ship**: a system its owner can no longer explain has already stopped being theirs, whatever the tests say. | PH-2 | superauditor round 3 |
| **AX-29** | 🟢 | **A live document states its present, and every line in it earns its place twice.** What it used to be, what was tried instead, why it is no longer done the old way: that is a decision's reasoning, and AX-24 gives it a home. **Both halves are written in the same act**, because a later pass can strip archaeology but cannot tell whether it was ever logged, and deleting unlogged history is the loss PH-3 forbids. Past tense survives in exactly one case: **naming a trap a reader can still fall into.** *"This used to be X"* is not that; *"this grep silently returns zero"* is. **And the two tests every line must pass:** *if the reader ignored it, would the work come out wrong?* **and** *could they have worked it out from the repository?* Only yes-then-no stays — the rest is methodology, and it belongs in the log.
